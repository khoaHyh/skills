# Story

The film is told from the material, not about it. Read the sources for what only they contain: the real post that started it, the question that keeps coming back, the number nobody expected, the stray tip that can become a prop.

## The metaphor

Pick one physical metaphor that every beat can happen inside. It gives each transition a cause and each joke a stage. Example: a team's chaotic launch week becomes a snowstorm of chat notifications the team digs out from under; a teammate's aside about using plastic shovels becomes the prop they dig with.

Test it by writing each beat as an action inside the metaphor ("the pings bury them", "they burst out with shovels"). A beat that only works as a caption over the metaphor means a weaker metaphor or a cut beat.

## The arc: escalation

Build in stages that each go further than the last, then cut to silence (the Hex demo runs normal phrases → nonsense → blast → cracked glass → rebuild). A 30 s grid at 120 BPM, one bar = 2 s:

| t (s) | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Open | The real artifact (the post, the commit, the ticket) on screen at frame 0 |
| 2–4 | Rise | It works: the thing launches, numbers roll up |
| 4–6 | Turn | The first problem hits, physically, and the title slams |
| 6–12 | Avalanche | Real items arrive on every beat, then twice a beat, then a blizzard |
| 12–14 | Build | Three or four shouted words with the quake ramping under a riser |
| 14–15 | Collapse | Hit-stop, flash, then a held beat of stillness with one small joke |
| 15–20 | Fight back | One action per beat, each with a receipt (a stamp, a commit, a counter) |
| 20–22 | Peak | The real numbers slam one per beat; the screen itself breaks |
| 22–26 | Silence | Flash cut to calm; the sweet line lands in the stillness |
| 26–27.5 | Button | The running gag returns |
| 27.5–30 | End card | Title, the next step, the mark; at least 2 s held |

Stretch or cut stages for other lengths; keep the turn, the peak and the silence.

## Facts and people

- Recompute every on-screen number from its source and list it in the treatment with where it came from.
- Quote verbatim when you quote; otherwise paraphrase and make it read as a paraphrase.
- Credit third parties by role or team, never by name, and keep customer or user data out entirely.
- Characters are the requester and the people they named in the brief.
- When the brief says public, list every internal detail on screen and ask before rendering.

## Copy

- Items on screen: one line each, about 80 characters at most.
- Slam words: 11 characters at most at 230 px on a 1920 frame.
- The sweet line: 8 words at most, on screen at least 2.5 s; the final line at least 2 s.
- Write the way the people in the material talk: real nouns, real numbers, contractions, complete sentences.

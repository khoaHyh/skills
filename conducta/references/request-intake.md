# Request intake

Treat the suggested fix as a hypothesis. The requester may lack product context, business requirements, invariants, implicit state machines, and the cost and inertia of change. Plain factual questions follow the normal answer path.

1. Restate what the person needs and why, separately from their suggested fix.
2. Investigate the evidence trail with available tools, within existing access grants, before fixing anything or asking them for more: current and past behavior, prior decisions, code, and data. Establish whether anything is actually wrong and whether the proposed intervention fits the need, including manual control versus automatic product behavior.
3. Prefer the durable, robust, minimal solution. Consider a short-term band-aid only when the requester is genuinely blocked and the durable fix cannot land in time. Label it a band-aid, bring both options to Khoa, and record the durable follow-up in the ledger before proceeding.

Khoa's direction and that of anyone he names outrank the requester's proposed design. Finish with an evidence-backed recommendation or an unresolved decision for Khoa. Existing action and communication authority still applies.
